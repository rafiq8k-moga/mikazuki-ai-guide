# Group Chat

Not every story fits two people. Through **Group Chat**, you can bring several characters into a single conversation at once, so they can take turns speaking, respond to each other, and take part in the same scene. This page explains how to create a group, how the system marks who is currently speaking, and the limitations you should know about. The characters' personalities themselves still follow the same data as in [Character Data and Memories](/en/fitur/character-data).

## Creating a Group Chat

The creation page is titled **"Create Group Chat"** and can be opened from the create-conversation area. Fill in the **group name** first if you like — optional, up to 60 characters, and when left empty the group is automatically named **"New Group"**. Then look up characters through the search box and tap them one by one to select them (a check mark and a counter like `3 selected` appear), then press **"Create Group (3)"** at the bottom.

**The requirements are simple: pick at least 2 and at most 255 characters** — the create button only becomes active when this is met. The on-screen hint sums it up directly: *"Select 2-255 characters. 1 message = 1 speaker."* If the character list is long, use **"Load more"** to keep browsing. Each member's distinguishing color is chosen automatically starting from the moment you check them on this page.

## How It Works

Every reply carries its speaker's identity: a **round avatar, a name, and that member's own color** appear above the bubble, so you always know who is talking even when the scene is busy. These colors are **unique per member**, drawn from a built-in palette of 10 colors that rotates again when the members outnumber the palette. This identity stays permanently attached to every message — even scrolling back to old messages, the speaker remains readable.

Conversation follows the principle **1 message = 1 speaker**: one of your messages is answered by one member, not all of them at once. You choose the speaker through the **avatar bar below the topbar** — the selected member gets a highlight ring, and the input placeholder changes to *"Reply as {name}..."*. Choose **"Random"** at the end of that bar if you would rather have the system decide who answers each of your messages.

You can still direct who replies even while Random mode is on. **Mention the name directly in your message**: a mention with an @ sign (for example `@Rion`) gets top priority — typing it even brings up a member list to pick from — and simply mentioning a member's name is detected too. When no name is detected, the speaker is chosen at random; the input hint reminds you of this: *"Mention a name to direct them, or use random selection."* The group can also be maintained through the **"Manage Group"** panel: renaming it, filling in a bio, adding new members, or removing members (the group still needs at least 2 members). The [Persona & System Note](/en/fitur/persona-system-note) space is also available and applies to the whole group.

## Limitations

A few things about group chat are deliberately kept simple. **Bond and hearts stay per character, not per group** — bond XP, level, and relationship status are attached to your chat with each individual character and don't pile up because they share one group (see Bond & Calendar). **The AI engine choice also follows your global settings** — system mode (Mikazuki) as well as BYOK set in Settings are used by the group as a whole; there is no separate per-group engine choice.

What is stored per group on your device includes: the name, the bio, the member list with its speaker colors, the wallpaper, the persona, and the system note. Because 1 message is only answered by 1 speaker, member-to-member interaction is best directed through name mentions, instead of hoping all the characters race to answer. With these two limitations understood, even big groups with dozens of characters remain comfortable to manage.
